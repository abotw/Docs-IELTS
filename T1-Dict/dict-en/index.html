    1. What Is an English Dictionary?

    An English dictionary is a reference tool that explains words in English.

    Depending on the type, it may tell you:

    • What a word means
    • How to pronounce it
    • How to spell it
    • How it is used in sentences
    • Its grammar, register, and collocations
    • Its origin and history

    Modern dictionaries are no longer just “word → meaning” lists — they are language learning tools.

    2. Main Types of English Dictionaries

    2.1 Monolingual vs Bilingual

    📘 Monolingual (English–English)

    • Definitions are written in English
    • Best for intermediate to advanced learners
    • Forces you to think in English

    Examples:

    • Oxford Advanced Learner’s Dictionary
    • Cambridge Dictionary
    • Longman Dictionary of Contemporary English

    📗 Bilingual (English–Chinese, English–Japanese, etc.)

    • Meanings translated into your native language
    • Good for beginners
    • Faster understanding, but less depth

    👉 Rule of thumb

    • Beginner → bilingual
    • Intermediate+ → monolingual (or both)

    2.2 Learner’s Dictionary vs Native Dictionary

    Learner’s Dictionary (Highly Recommended)

    Designed specifically for learners:

    • Simple definitions (limited vocabulary)
    • Clear grammar labels
    • Many example sentences
    • Usage notes and common mistakes

    Examples:

    • Oxford Advanced Learner’s Dictionary
    • Longman Dictionary of Contemporary English (LDOCE)

    Native (General) Dictionary

    Designed for native speakers:

    • Complex definitions
    • Fewer examples
    • More encyclopedic

    Examples:

    • Merriam-Webster (unabridged)
    • Oxford English Dictionary (OED)

    ⚠️ Beginners should avoid the OED — it’s for linguists, not learners.

    3. Print vs Online vs App Dictionaries

    3.1 Print Dictionaries

    Pros

    • Focused reading
    • No distractions

    Cons

    • Heavy
    • Quickly outdated
    • No audio

    3.2 Online Dictionaries (Most Popular)

    Pros

    • Free
    • Always updated
    • Audio pronunciation
    • Searchable

    Cons

    • Easy to skim without learning

    Popular choices:

    • Cambridge Dictionary
    • Oxford Learner’s Dictionaries
    • Longman Online

    3.3 Dictionary Apps

    Pros

    • Offline access
    • Bookmarking
    • Word lists

    Cons

    • Quality varies by app

    4. Anatomy of a Dictionary Entry (Very Important)

    A dictionary entry contains much more than a definition.

    Typical Parts Explained

    1. Headword The word being defined.
    2. Pronunciation
      • IPA symbols: /ˈwɜːd/
      • Often both UK and US
    3. Part of Speech
      • noun (n.)
      • verb (v.)
      • adjective (adj.)
      • adverb (adv.)
    4. Definition(s)
      • Numbered meanings
      • Ordered by frequency, not logic
    5. Example Sentences
      • Real, natural usage
      • Extremely important for learners
    6. Grammar Information
      • Countable / uncountable
      • Transitive / intransitive
      • Verb patterns
    7. Collocations
      • Words that commonly go together (e.g. make a decision, not do a decision)
    8. Usage Labels
      • formal / informal
      • spoken / written
      • British / American
      • slang / taboo

    5. How to Look Up a Word Correctly

    Many learners make the same mistake: they read only the first definition.

    Correct Lookup Steps

    1. Identify the part of speech (Is it a noun or a verb here?)
    2. Read all definitions briefly
      • Skip the wrong ones
    3. Check example sentences
      • Does it match your context?
    4. Notice grammar & collocations
      • Especially for verbs and nouns
    5. Listen to pronunciation
      • Out loud, not silently

    6. Common Beginner Mistakes

    ❌ Only memorizing definitions

    ❌ Ignoring example sentences

    ❌ Translating word-by-word

    ❌ Using rare or formal meanings in daily speech

    ❌ Not checking part of speech

    ✅ Good learners:

    • Learn words in sentences
    • Learn how words behave, not just what they mean

    7. How to Use a Dictionary for Learning (Not Just Checking)

    7.1 Active Dictionary Use

    • Write down example sentences
    • Replace words in examples with your own
    • Notice repeated patterns

    7.2 Build a Personal Word List

    For each word, record:

    • Word
    • Part of speech
    • One clear meaning
    • One real sentence

    7.3 Combine with Spaced Repetition

    • Put dictionary examples into Anki
    • Review usage, not translations

    8. Recommended Dictionaries (Learner-Friendly)

    Best Overall

    • Oxford Advanced Learner’s Dictionary
    • Longman Dictionary of Contemporary English

    Best Free Online

    • Cambridge Dictionary
    • Longman Online

    Best for Pronunciation

    • Cambridge (clear UK/US audio)

    9. Final Advice

    A dictionary is not a translation tool.

    It is a map of how words live in a language.

    If you use dictionaries to:

    • Understand context
    • Observe patterns
    • Learn usage, not just meaning

    then your English will become natural, accurate, and confident.
