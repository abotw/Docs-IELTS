## 1. What Is LDOCE?

**LDOCE** stands for **Longman Dictionary of Contemporary English**.

It is one of the most authoritative **learner’s English dictionaries**, designed especially for:

-   ESL / EFL learners
-   Students preparing for exams (IELTS, TOEFL, CET, etc.)
-   Anyone who wants **clear definitions and real usage examples**

Unlike native-speaker dictionaries, LDOCE is **written in simple English** and focuses on **how English is actually used today**.

## 2. Why LDOCE Is Special

### 2.1 Controlled Defining Vocabulary (Key Advantage)

LDOCE uses about **2,000 basic English words** to explain all other words.

**Why this matters:**

-   You can understand definitions **without looking up more words**
-   Definitions are short, precise, and learner-friendly

**Example:**

>   *abandon* — *to leave someone or something and never return*

No complex grammar terms, no obscure vocabulary.

### 2.2 Corpus-Based, Real English

LDOCE is built on the **Longman Corpus Network**, which contains billions of real English words from:

-   Conversations
-   News
-   Academic writing
-   TV & movies

This ensures:

-   Examples sound **natural**
-   Meanings reflect **modern usage**
-   Frequency information is accurate

### 2.3 Clear Sense Organization

Each entry is carefully structured:

-   Meanings are **numbered**
-   Each sense has **its own examples**
-   Different parts of speech are separated

This helps you avoid confusing similar meanings.

## 3. Understanding an LDOCE Entry

Let’s break down a typical entry.

### 3.1 Headword & Pronunciation

-   **British & American pronunciation**
-   IPA symbols
-   Often includes **audio**

Example:

```
record /ˈrekɔːd/ (noun)
record /rɪˈkɔːd/ (verb)
```

### 3.2 Part of Speech

Each meaning clearly shows:

-   noun / verb / adjective / adverb
-   countable (C) / uncountable (U)

Example:

```
record (C)
```

This is crucial for correct grammar.

### 3.3 Definitions (Simple English)

Definitions are:

-   Short
-   Concrete
-   Written using basic vocabulary

### 3.4 Example Sentences

Examples are:

-   Real (from corpus)
-   Simple but natural
-   Focused on common usage

Example:

>   She **kept a record** of all her expenses.

### 3.5 Collocations (Very Important)

LDOCE shows **which words commonly go together**.

Example:

-   **keep a record**
-   **break a record**
-   **criminal record**

This helps you sound natural instead of “textbook English”.

## 4. Grammar & Usage Support

### 4.1 Grammar Labels

You’ll often see notes like:

-   [I] intransitive
-   [T] transitive
-   [+ of / + that / + -ing]

Example:

```
suggest + that-clause
suggest + -ing
```

### 4.2 Usage Notes

LDOCE warns you about:

-   Common mistakes
-   Confusing words
-   Register differences (formal vs informal)

Example:

>   **Note:** *say* is not used with an object + infinitive.

## 5. Frequency & Importance Indicators

### 5.1 Frequency Stars

Many entries include stars:

-   ★★★ very common
-   ★★ common
-   ★ less common

This helps you prioritize:

>   Learn ★★★ words first.

### 5.2 Spoken vs Written English

LDOCE often tells you:

-   More common in spoken English
-   Mostly used in formal writing

This is extremely useful for exams and real-life communication.

## 7. How to Use LDOCE Effectively

### 7.1 Don’t Translate Immediately

When you look up a word:

1.  Read the **English definition**
2.  Check **examples**
3.  Note **collocations**
4.  Only translate if necessary

### 7.2 Learn in Chunks, Not Single Words

Instead of memorizing:

>   *decision*

Learn:

-   **make a decision**
-   **reach a decision**
-   **a difficult decision**

This is how native speakers think.

### 7.3 Combine with Anki or Notes

Recommended workflow:

-   Look up word in LDOCE
-   Copy:
    -   Definition
    -   One good example
    -   Key collocation
-   Put into Anki or notes

## 9. Who Should Use LDOCE?

LDOCE is ideal if you:

-   Are an **intermediate or advanced learner**
-   Want to **think in English**
-   Care about **natural usage**
-   Prepare for **IELTS / TOEFL / CET**

## 10. Final Advice

Think of LDOCE not as a “dictionary”, but as:

>   **A guide to how modern English really works.**

If you use it consistently and correctly, your vocabulary will become:

-   More accurate
-   More natural
-   More confident
