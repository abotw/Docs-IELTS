## What Is AWL?

**AWL** stands for the **Academic Word List**. It is a carefully selected list of academic vocabulary words that frequently appear in academic texts across different disciplines such as science, economics, education, and social studies.

The AWL was developed by **Averil Coxhead** at Victoria University of Wellington in 2000. It is widely used by English learners, teachers, and exam candidates to build strong academic vocabulary.

# 1. Why Is AWL Important?

If you are preparing for:

-   IELTS
-   TOEFL
-   Academic reading or writing
-   University study in English

AWL is extremely important because:

✅ These words appear frequently in academic texts

✅ They are NOT common everyday words

✅ They help improve writing band score

✅ They increase reading speed and comprehension

For IELTS Writing (especially Task 2), using AWL vocabulary correctly can help you:

-   Improve **Lexical Resource**
-   Write more formal and academic essays
-   Avoid basic vocabulary repetition

# 2. Structure of the Academic Word List

The AWL contains:

-   **570 word families**
-   Divided into **10 sublists**
-   Ordered by frequency (Sublist 1 = most frequent)

### Example Structure

| Sublist    | Frequency Level  | Example Words                   |
| ---------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Sublist 1  | Most frequent    | analyse, approach, area, assess |
| Sublist 5  | Medium frequency | pursue, react, regime           |
| Sublist 10 | Less frequent    | adjacent, forthcoming           |

A *word family* includes different forms of a word:

Example:

-   analyse
-   analysis
-   analyst
-   analytical

You only need to learn the root + common forms.

# 3. How AWL Was Created

**Averil Coxhead** built the list by analyzing:

-   3.5 **million** words
-   Academic texts from:
    -   Arts
    -   Commerce
    -   Law
    -   Science

She removed:

-   Basic high-frequency words (like “the”, “and”, “people”)
-   Words already included in general high-frequency lists

The result: words that are specifically useful for academic study.

# 4. Examples of Important AWL Words

Here are some high-frequency AWL words that are extremely useful for IELTS:

### 🔹 Analyse

To examine in detail.

>   The graph **analyses** the changes in population over time.

### 🔹 Establish

To create or prove something.

>   The government must **establish** clear regulations.

### 🔹 Significant

Important or noticeable.

>   There was a **significant** increase in employment.

### 🔹 Assume

To accept something as true without proof.

>   It is often **assumed** that technology improves productivity.

### 🔹 Interpret

To explain meaning.

>   The data can be **interpreted** in several ways.

# 5. AWL vs General Vocabulary

| General English | Academic English (AWL) |
| --------------- | ---------------------- |
| get             | obtain                 |
| show            | demonstrate            |
| help            | assist                 |
| start           | initiate               |
| use             | utilise                |

In academic writing, the right column is preferred.

But ⚠️ remember:

Do NOT replace every simple word. Overuse looks unnatural.

# 6. How to Study AWL Effectively

Since you are preparing for IELTS, here is a practical strategy:

### Step 1: Study by Sublist

Start from Sublist 1 → Sublist 2 → Sublist 3.

### Step 2: Learn Word Families

Example:

-   contribute
-   contribution
-   contributor

### Step 3: Learn Collocations

Instead of memorizing single words:

❌ make analysis

✅ conduct analysis

❌ big impact

✅ significant impact

### Step 4: Use in Writing Practice

When writing Task 2 essays:

-   Replace basic vocabulary gradually
-   Do not force complex words

# 7. Common Mistakes Students Make

❌ Memorizing without context

❌ Using words incorrectly

❌ Overusing difficult vocabulary

❌ Ignoring grammar of word forms

Example mistake:

>   This policy will *significantly influence to* society. ❌

Correct:

>   This policy will *significantly influence* society. ✅

# 8. AWL and IELTS Band Score

To reach:

-   **Band 6** → Some academic vocabulary
-   **Band 7** → Flexible and accurate use
-   **Band 8+** → Natural, precise, sophisticated use

AWL is not enough alone — but it is a strong foundation.

# 9. Is AWL Enough for IELTS?

Not completely.

You also need:

-   Topic-specific vocabulary (environment, education, crime)
-   Paraphrasing skills
-   Grammar accuracy
-   Cohesion devices

But AWL gives you the academic core vocabulary.

# 10. Advanced Insight (For Serious Learners)

Modern research sometimes compares AWL with the newer:

**Academic Vocabulary List (AVL)**

The AVL is corpus-based and more updated.

However:

AWL is still widely used in IELTS preparation materials.
